Strategy Foundations | 2026

Blue Ocean Strategy

by Kim & Mauborgne

Why it matters: Competing in the same market with the same offering is a losing game. Kim and Mauborgne argue the real opportunity is creating uncontested market space, what they call a blue ocean, where competition becomes irrelevant.

Study this if: You build products, lead growth, or set direction for an organization looking to escape margin compression and find new demand.

What you will gain: A systematic approach to creating new market space, principles drawn from a study of one hundred and fifty strategic moves across a century, and tools for making the competition irrelevant.

Good Strategy | Bad Strategy

by Richard P. Rumelt

Why it matters: Most documents called strategy are vague goals, slogans, and financial targets, not strategy at all. Richard Rumelt clarifies the muddled thinking that produces weak plans and lays out what real, coherent, action-oriented strategy actually looks like.

Study this if: You write strategic plans, evaluate them, or want a sharper standard for separating real strategy from corporate filler.

What you will gain: A clear definition of good and bad strategy, the kernel that sits at the center of every strong plan, and nine sources of strategic power you can apply across business, nonprofit, and government work.

by Krogerus & Techappeler

Study this if: You want a working reference for SWOT, the Eisenhower Matrix, the Prisoner's Dilemma, and the other classic models that sharpen real decisions.

What you will gain: Fifty illustrated decision models for strategic thinking, a compact reference for personal and professional choices, and a practical toolkit for simplifying complex problems.

Noise

by Daniel Kahneman

Why it matters: Two doctors give different diagnoses for the same patient. Two judges hand down different sentences for the same crime. Daniel Kahneman, Olivier Sibony, and Cass Sunstein show that variability in human judgment, what they call noise, costs organizations far more than people realize.

Study this if: You make consequential judgments, build hiring or evaluation processes, or run any system where consistent decisions matter.

What you will gain: A clear framework for identifying noise alongside bias, practical strategies for reducing both, and a more disciplined approach to judgment in any field.
